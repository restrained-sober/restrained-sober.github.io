research

The questions and methods behind my work.

My research asks what it means for an AI system to be a reliable teacher or explanatory partner. Correct answers are only one part of the problem: a system must also respond to the learner, maintain professional boundaries, and avoid unfair judgments.

The homepage offers a visual overview of selected papers. This page explains the research questions and evaluation approaches. Full bibliographic records are available on Google Scholar.

01 · Professional fidelity and safety

Question. When an LLM adopts the role of a teacher, does it behave like a competent and ethically reliable professional—or merely sound like one?

Approach. My co-first-authored work examines this from two complementary perspectives. EduGuardBench evaluates pedagogical role fidelity alongside adversarial safety, distinguishing teaching failures from responses to harmful requests. EMNLP studies teacher-role models through personality profiling, professional moral dilemmas, and soft-prompt attacks.

Why it matters. Professional evaluation needs to look beyond fluency and factual accuracy. It must test how a model handles uncertainty, ethically difficult situations, and pressure to depart from its role.

EduGuardBench · AAAI 2026 · EMNLP · EMNLP 2025

02 · Multimodal fairness and human–AI evaluation

Question. How do images influence an AI system’s judgments, and how can human interaction reveal limitations that final-answer scoring misses?

Approach. Edu-MMBias, which I co-first-authored, audits cognitive, affective, and behavioral bias in educational vision-language models. ArtMentor, which I co-authored, studies multimodal capabilities in artwork recognition, critique, and suggestions through a human–AI interaction system.

Why it matters. These projects examine different problems: fairness in educational judgments and model capability in art evaluation. Both move beyond treating a single generated answer as the whole evaluation target.

Edu-MMBias · Preprint, 2026 · ArtMentor · CHI 2025

03 · Simulated learners and interactive assessment

Question. Can an AI system notice what a learner has not understood, adapt its next explanation, and check whether understanding has improved?

Current interests. I am interested in simulated learners with differing knowledge states, multi-turn evaluation, and the consistency of learner behavior over time. My research experience connects these questions with close analysis of failure cases rather than relying only on aggregate scores.

Related work. In SimComm, my first-authored work presented orally at AERA 2025, I studied multi-agent teacher communication training through a quasi-experiment and eye tracking. This addresses human teacher training, a related but distinct problem from evaluating an AI tutor.
